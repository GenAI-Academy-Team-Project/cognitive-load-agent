+CaresteadHOW IT WORKS

Carestead decision path

Carestead · GenAI Academy · September 2026 · v109 / 12
Authenticate and scope, retrieve records, then check evidence. Insufficient evidence leads to clarification or refusal. Questions receive grounded answers. Actions pass policy and caregiver approval, then scoped execution, outcome verification, audit, and scoring. Blocked, declined, or timed-out actions make no change. This is the supplied conceptual decision path; the evaluation slide retains the earlier captured benchmark.
+CaresteadBOUNDED AUTONOMY

The agent proposes.
Fixed rules and people control the action.

Agent decides

Relevant records, evidence sufficiency, risk severity and rationale, and a supported next step.

Fixed controls enforce

Recipient access, consent, allowed tools, validation, approval rules, and integration scope.

Caregiver decides

Approve or cancel the exact change; connect services; accept a practical care plan.

Every action is traced. Never diagnose, change doses, judge a care interruption medically safe, or dismiss emergency care.

Source: supplied team handout · Tools, pp. 5–6 · Q3, pp. 7–910 / 12
Verified against the supplied MAI #41-Aug 2026 Team_Breakout_Handout.pdf: Tools and Actions on pages 5–6 and Q3 on pages 7–9. The current decision engine is deterministic. The agent does not receive arbitrary database access. Access and relevant preconditions are rechecked at execution; failures must be reported without claiming success. Q3 also reserves data export/deletion decisions for the caregiver and puts retention/deletion rules under fixed controls. Good outcomes require the right evidence, decision, policy, action, and verified result. Traces expose safety, quality, policy, and outcome-verification failures; stale or contradictory evidence requires safe handling.
